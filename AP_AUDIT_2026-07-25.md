# Fieldhorse v2 — Financial Audit (July 25, 2026)

Fourth audit pass on this codebase (after `FIELDHORSE_AUDIT.md`, `AUDIT_REPORT.md`,
and PR #204's 75-finding pass). Trigger: "numbers are still off." Scope: every
surface that computes, displays, or sends a dollar figure — receivables (A/R),
payables (subs/crew labor), dashboards, analytics, customer documents, the
Netlify functions behind them, and the database layer.

Method: five parallel deep-read audits (money-math core, financial UI, customer
documents/PDF, database/backend, payables/timesheets), findings cross-verified
against the code, then fixed. Baseline before this pass: typecheck, lint, 110
unit tests, and the production build were all green — every problem below was
behavioral, invisible to CI.

## Root causes found (all fixed in this pass)

### 1. Five disagreeing definitions of "outstanding balance"
`rollups.ts` (Clients list, ClientDetail) counted only `job/invoice` stages and
ignored approved change orders; the Invoices screen, client statements, and the
job Financials tab counted `closed` jobs and added COs. A job closed with money
still owed — or fully paid on the base contract with an unpaid approved CO —
showed **$0 owed on one screen and the real balance on another**, including on
two widgets of the same ClientDetail screen. All surfaces now share one
definition: stages `job/invoice/closed`, contract = amount + approved COs,
balances ≤ $0.50 ignored. "Lifetime" value no longer counts lost bids.

### 2. Home dashboard treated "payments since Sunday" as all payments
The dashboard fetched only the current week's payments and used them as total
payment history: fully-paid jobs showed "Chase invoice · $25,000 owed" (the
full contract, ignoring even partial payments), Job Health read "Outstanding"
for jobs paid last month, and "Ready to invoice" labeled money already
collected. It now fetches full payment history (paged), chases only real
balances, shows the remaining amount owed, folds in approved COs, and keys
"collected this week" on `paid_on`.

### 3. One draw invoice, three different "amount due" figures
For a $2,500 deposit draw on a $10,000 job: the email subject said $2,500, the
public link said $2,500 — and the attached **PDF hero band said "Amount due
$10,000"** (the whole remaining contract). The public page's "Pay now" button
also quoted the whole balance above a $2,500 hero. The PDF hero, the pay
button, the InvoiceDetail document view, and its email path now all bill the
current open draw (capped at the balance); jobs without draw rows keep the
whole-balance presentation. Whole-dollar display rounding no longer hides real
cents anywhere ($12,499.50 no longer renders "$12,500" next to a cent-precise
table).

### 4. Money-entry and rounding bugs that wrote wrong values
- Draw editor accepted "1,500" / "$1,500", passed `NaN` validation, and
  **persisted a $0 draw**. Input now sanitized + validated like the sibling
  money inputs.
- `suggestNextInvoice`'s `unbilled || balance` fallthrough prefilled a
  duplicate invoice for the full outstanding balance the moment everything was
  billed; whole-dollar `Math.round` left invoice sets that never reconciled to
  contracts carrying cents. Now cents-accurate and $0 when nothing is unbilled.
- Payment idempotency key was reused after a failed attempt even when the
  operator corrected the amount — the retry silently no-op'd while the UI
  claimed the corrected amount was recorded. Key now re-mints when the payload
  changes.
- Auto-close on payment re-reads the contract amount instead of trusting the
  caller's cached row.

### 5. Crew labor silently cost $0 — then retroactively repriced
- Migration 034 removed the RLS policy that let the owner's browser read crew
  rates, so unrated punches priced at **$0 labor** (the exact bug migration 057
  claimed to fix). Migration 058 restores owner/admin/manager read.
- Punches carried no rate snapshot, so a raise repriced every historical (even
  approved, even closed-job) shift. Rates now snapshot at clock-out and
  backfill at approval.
- `recalcCost` summed only the *viewer's* subs/expenses and no-op'd its write
  for non-owners — an admin's $6,000 sub never entered cached job cost, and
  cost flip-flopped depending on who touched the job last. It now sums all org
  rows and resolves the true job owner.
- Crew clock-out and timesheet approval now trigger a cost recalc (margins used
  to sit stale until the owner touched an unrelated expense).
- Timesheets approval screen showed $0 estimated cost for exactly the punches
  that bill the job (no snapshot rate); it now prices them with the same
  default-rate fallback job costing uses. Flagged (disputed) punches no longer
  bill the job, no longer count in KPIs, and are excluded from "Approve all".
- Crew Home double-counted the open shift ("Today on the clock 6:00" three
  hours in).

### 6. Sub portal showed the GC's revenue as the sub's income (and leaked it)
`fh_payments` rows are client→contractor receipts, but the portal summed them
as the sub's "Paid YTD" and listed every payment's amount/method/reference to
any partner on the job. A homeowner's $40,000 draw appeared as the drywall
sub's income. Payments (and the GC's contract amounts) are no longer returned
to the portal at all; the payments card and Paid YTD KPI are gone until a real
per-sub ledger exists.

### 7. Analytics that drifted on every edit
- Won YTD / Profit YTD / "Invoiced YTD" keyed on `updated_at` — fixing a typo
  on last year's $50K job re-booked it into this year. Now anchored to the
  stage-transition log (win date), with `updated_at` only as legacy fallback.
- Profit YTD clamped per-job losses to zero, overstating profit by every
  over-budget job.
- The funnel query kept the *oldest* 4,000 transitions, starving the
  trailing-90-day funnel of exactly the rows it needed; win-rate had three
  different definitions on one screen; a contact lost-then-won double-counted
  in the denominator. All unified.
- Date-only columns (`paid_on`, `due_at`) were UTC-parsed in several
  aggregations, dropping Jan-1/month-boundary payments into the prior
  period and flipping "Past due" the evening before the due date. All local
  parses now.

### 8. Silent 1000-row truncation of "uncapped" financial queries
Supabase/PostgREST caps responses at 1,000 rows even with no `.limit()`. The
A/R payments query (whose comment says "deliberately UNCAPPED … must see every
row"), the Clients bundle, the Analytics bundle, and the Home dashboard all
lost their oldest rows past 1,000 — paid understated, outstanding overstated,
silently. All aggregate fetches now page via `.range()`.

### 9. Customer documents: terms/warranty swap, expired-quote approval, snapshot gaps
- The proposal's "Payment terms" section always showed a hardcoded 50/40/10
  deposit schedule while the contractor's actual terms text rendered under
  "Warranty". Customers approved against deposit terms the contractor never
  set. Now wired correctly on both the public page and in-app preview.
- A customer could open an old link and legally-bind approve an **expired**
  proposal at stale pricing (`public-link-approve` never checked
  `quote_expires_at`). Now rejected after the validity date.
- The approval snapshot dropped items whose `amount` was null (no qty×rate
  fallback), under-recording the approved contract vs the document shown.
- The public JSON payload shipped draft/void invoice rows and internal note
  columns; the in-app preview showed draft draws the customer would never see.
  Both now match the PDF's customer-visible set.

### 10. Smaller fixes
Sub identity keying (formatted vs digits-only phone split one sub into
multiple cards with partial totals; "untitled" cards dead-ended); client LTV
trigger still summed the retired `'invoice'` stage (migration 059 + backfill);
InvoiceDetail aged receivables from job creation (red "Overdue · 91d" on a job
first billed yesterday — now anchored like the A/R list); Financials "Invoice"
badge showed the payments count; InvoiceDetail draw cache went stale after a
settling payment; email money labels rounded away real cents.

## Verification
- `npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint`: clean.
- `npm test`: 127 passed (110 baseline + 17 new tests covering the unified
  rollup definition, CO handling, dashboard paid-state, YTD anchoring, invoice
  suggestions, and sub identity keying).
- `npm run build`: clean.

## Known items deliberately NOT changed (candidates for a next pass)
- **No overtime logic anywhere** — all hours are straight-time. If crew is
  paid OT after 40, payroll is understated on every screen.
- `break_minutes` exists in the schema and all math honors it, but no UI sets
  it.
- Overpayments/credits clamp to $0 on statements and rollups — a client's
  credit on one property never offsets a debt on another, and an overpaid
  invoice shows "PAID" with no credit line.
- The migration-011 trigger recomputes `fh_contacts.amount` from quote items
  org-wide (RLS bypassed) and snaps back manual amount edits after any item
  tweak; the quote screen shows only the viewer's items. Changing trigger
  semantics risks the quote flow — needs its own design pass.
- Owner TimeClockCard can book the expense even when the punch close fails
  (split ledgers on flaky connections).
- Org-role posture: after migration 034, crew-role members have org-wide
  read/write on payments/invoices/contacts. Policy choice, not a bug — flagged
  for the owner to confirm.
- No CHECK constraints on payment/invoice sign; a negative payment flows into
  sums (may be intentional for refunds).
